1. Talk about a time where you had to accept or seek help for a problem.

When I was working on an online checkout system for the CometCupboard, I was tasked by our team lead with making an online queue for the check in process. 
This queue system involved modifying the permissions of the user during different stages of the queue so that they could only access the relevant content for their place in line.
I had no experience at this time with working on permissions but did my best to create a working queue system.
While I created something that worked on my machine, it didn't work anywhere else and so I had to go to the team lead who walked me through what I had done well and wrong.
I was shown how my idea was correct but where I had been working was wrong and that I could solve the problem much more simply by modifying existing permission setters.
As a result, the way I think about reusing and modifying functions is different now and I believe I am all the better for it.

4. Describe how you handle problems were the solution is not immediately obvious.

I have had several projects both in an academic setting and project based setting where this has applied. 
For example, one of my classes wanted us to create what amount to a breadth first search using stacks to find the shortest path between two points. 
At the time, I was newer to programming and did not understand how to approach this and how to apply stacks to this problem.
However, when faced with a problem I do not understand, I make some solution first and then refine it, thinking around what already exists.
I believe my first solution was to try passing the whole stack down the path and then back up again which didn't work.
Eventually, thinking both from starting at the front and the end with my solution I arrived at the correct answer. 
Essentially, I always try to have something written and the refine it by seeing where the initial solution fails.

6. Tell me about a time where your solution to a problem didn't work out. What happened and what was your response?

For my senior capstone project, a Road Report AI, I was tasked with data Engineering. 
I was to take a bunch of crash data and weather data and return history for when car crashes did and did not happen.
For the weather I used a source that took weather airports as they were recording weather the had for sure happened as opposed to speculation.
I used this for a while and it took a lot of preprocessing to get to work the way I needed it to. However, I did not test how broad the source was.
Eventually I came to the conclusion that it would not provide the amount of data I needed ot to provide, only one location for an extremely large area.
This was not useable and I had to pivot to a source called OpenMeteo that provided any feasible location but was more speculative.
This boosted the ability of the model to predict and taught me about testing my tools before I begin a task.

7. Discuss a time where you needed to meet a deadline and how you handled it.

For my senior capstone project I was the lead data engineer working to build training data for the AI model that would predict road safety.
The training data included automating the transformation of crash data, getting third party weather data, transforming weather data, creating negative samples, and then reducing the data for all of Texas.
While initially I was confident in my ability to produce all of these things, scope creep and technical issues arose. 
With three weeks left, I had only managed to make the rough draft of training data with a already pre-formatted crash data but appropriate weather data.
At this time, I realized I needed to prioritize what I could get done before the deadline beyond what should be done. 
I ended up prioritizing a workable MVP for the presentation that was due which meant I had to cut a lot of scope including expanding the System for all of Texas and cutting the large amount of data generated.
While it was not the most fleshed out system, it worked for the project and we received a good enough grade for the project. It taught me to get done with what is essential and then expand.
